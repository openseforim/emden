These things I did and did not leave them, "to beautify the house of my sanctuary" (Isa. 60:13). And further, as I wished, in my "House of God" I included many moral teachings, which I poured out, since they are much needed to arouse hearts, especially of our brethren the Ashkenazim: to keep them far from the matters of the gentiles and to remove from them every blemish, so as to bring them to the happiness of success, to make them happy. But men who hate rebuke "repaid me evil" (Ps. 35:12) in return for my pursuing their good and their true benefit.

And behold, at first a name went out at once for this work, and it found favour in the eyes of all who saw it, until whoever saw it, "while it was yet in his hand he swallowed it" (Isa. 28:4); and almost as soon as the first part of it was finished, they already longed to buy it. And it was this that troubled me. For through this zealots fell upon me, "a brood of sinful men" (Num. 32:14), who "laid wanton charges against me" (Deut. 22:14); for they searched and found that I had written, in the laws of conduct and of commerce, what the sages, of blessed memory, said in the Mishnah, that a man should not teach his son to be a shopkeeper; and I had stealthily added some words in my own words: "And woe [to the money-changers] in these generations" [a]. By this they were filled with fury and wrath, and the anger of the money-changers, brazen-faced, [was such that] they almost thought to swallow me for nothing. They raged so, and said with full mouth, as I heard from behind truthful men, that if they met me they would lay hands on me; and "blessed be the Lord, who delivered the soul of the needy from the hand of evildoers" (Jer. 20:13)!

Indeed, these "sinners against their own souls" (Num. 17:3) bound themselves in a conspiracy of the wicked against me, and came as one man to the chamber of the community by day, and roused the parnassim with outcry and great commotion, saying that I was bringing them into danger. They spoke much evil of me, and asked them to command that the printed book be taken from my hand and the page torn out of it; and to cover the nakedness of their shame with the garment of traitors, "the proud forged a lie against me" (Ps. 119:69), that many things were found in this book that cause danger to all Jewry. They also complained about two of the readings of the prayer texts in it. All this they did to multiply the shrill chatter against me; and the parnassim acceded to them, to seat R. Y. Ḥ. and the court to look into this book of mine, whether they could find a pretext against me.

Yet I had already gone ahead myself: immediately after the end of the first part, called Ammudei Shamayim, I had sent it to the hand of the said old head of the court to obtain an approbation from him, as is the custom. And I made a condition, intending not to take an approbation from him at the beginning of the printing on the strength of a page or two that he had seen, as is the custom of most authors of books in this generation; I did not want that, but only that he should testify from what he had seen of most of it, as of all of it. The said first part was in his hand several weeks, to study it all, before he gave me the approbation. Afterwards he sent it into my hand with praise and the glory of the signature by his hand, except that I saw that he had cut short somewhat the merits of the work. So there were in my hands two signed approbations and the licence of the king, may his majesty be exalted, for the printing, so that it was not enough to ... against them (?), that they should not obtain, by order of the parnassim through messengers, a protest in my hands against finishing the printing before they gave permission especially for it. When they did so, through their beadle, to make known to me that they had decreed on me as said, they wanted me to hand over from my books six copies of this first part that was finished, to deliver them to a doubled court: that is, six that they chose, three added to the fixed court of three, besides the head of the court, so that one book would be in the hand of each one of them, to study in it all they needed. They did not set a time for the matter, when the thing would come to an end and conclusion; each one of them took it to his house to examine as far as his hand reached, and by their word the judgment of the book would be given.

Although I said: behold, I already have in my hand a double approbation from the old head of the court, which he had in his hand already, and how could he pull down what he had built? — or would the others be able to answer in a quarrel? — my words of self-defence did not help. And though I could have laughed at them, I did not want to quarrel with the many, and I made myself ready to fulfil their request. I handed over the books they wanted from me into the hand of their beadle, with an answer by word of mouth: although I am not obliged in this, behold, I do this willingly, and the examining judges will give me satisfaction if they look into it with fine care; and where I have erred, let them make me understand, and let them blot it out of my book, and I will be grateful to them [for good] if they save me from the stumbling that is under my hand, God forbid. Therefore say to them in my name, as a request, that they should not stumble over it, and it will be counted to me for good, "for in these things I delight" (Jer. 9:23).

In truth that day was hard for me, when the said sinners "prepared war" (Joel 4:9) against me; perhaps they would go down with me to my life, for I had spent several thousands on a holy and great work, and how much sorrow and weariness I had until the first part was finished and most of the second near to completion! It was almost for nothing, God forbid. I greatly feared the accusers in this work of the Lord, from which a great name had gone out for me, "and it did not cease" (Deut. 5:19), lest, God forbid, it should not come to completion. Therefore I made haste the next day to return to the work, and commanded my workman to hold fast to his work and not let go until it was all completed, with God's help. And so it was done, without hindrance or further mishap. For the opponents had thought that there was still time to hold up the spread of the book in the world, once they found something they wanted with the judges examining my book, who they were sure would find what they wished, evil enough and more, in a short time. Indeed, out of worry over a hindrance, God forbid, I made many abbreviations in the last half of the second part, called Sha'arei Shamayim and Ir ha-Elohim, to include many words under one abbreviation. So quick was I this time in the work of this composition, so that no further delay or hindrance should come into my hand; and this is what caused difficulty of understanding in places of its said last half. In the meantime and after it — which was more than half a year, for in the week in which the Ninth of Av fell [a] this hard event befell me — until near the end of the month [of Shevat] [5]509 [1749] the said judges had not finished their work, who sat and examined to know the judgment of the book and its deed. All this time I kept sending my messenger to the head of the court and to the parnassim, that they should let me know whether they had found in it any trace of a thing, so that "I should not let iniquity dwell in my tents" (Job 11:14), God forbid. But I could not get to the bottom of their manner, for each one pushed me off to the other. And I understood that, although they had made some objections against me according to their opinion and their mistake, or as the plotters had asked of them — for they were their hired men without doubt — even so they were afraid of me, and did not trust themselves that their words would stand against me; therefore they hid from me what they had written to weaken the force of the book. The parnassim too kept silent and made themselves as if they did not know; though several householders bought the book from me, they did not protest at all. As it seemed, they were regretting it and thinking, "Would that they had not begun with me"; for the parnassim knew and felt the weakness of the things written against me, and worried lest no honour come to them, but only disgrace and shame to the court in general; for they had tested its strength and understood that they could not stand against me. Therefore they chose silence, for the sake of honour: they said neither forbidden nor permitted, and left me to sell and the householders to buy, with no one to protest or object.

Also, at once after the event, some of the money-changers themselves came and bought the first part of the book from me at a high price; and so great was the fear of me upon them that at once their anger cooled somewhat. The head of the ruffians too, Aaron Berliner, sent to ask my forgiveness. But my mind was not set at rest by their silence, and I did not stop sending to the parnassim week after week that they give me the objections of the judges: I would know whether there is substance in their words, and I would set right what was crooked; and if there is iniquity in me and a guilt known to me, as the law is, then I would bow my head to do according to the law and the Torah; and if I am clean of all blame and fault, they cannot make me forget it: until when shall this be a snare to me? All this stirred me to do some small thing, just to arouse the heart of the upright; perhaps I would merit to lessen the stumbling. And in the way the sages, of blessed memory, made mention of the shopkeeper and his fellows, although it is impossible to abolish them from the world — and what is the difference between a shopkeeper and a money-changer? Are they not brothers? "A trader, the balances of deceit are in his hand; he loves to oppress" (Hos. 12:8): these with kinds of food and those with silver and gold. How much more now, when these latter have made their deeds worse, and their evil touches the public with real danger to life — all the more must one mention and arouse them. Yet for all that I did not touch, even by a hair's breadth, the honour of any individual among them, for surely there are honoured men among them too, as in all crafts. But those known robbers in this burnt-out craft, "the thief whose hat burns", made all that great and mighty commotion against me. And although they could not take away the force of my book from its strength and its good name, even so they produced bitter fruit, to damage my purse; for all my toil and labour and the many expenses I spent for the delight of Heaven went for nothing. Besides this, the money-changers made a treacherous conspiracy among themselves not to buy my books; and they also turned away the heart of the people in general, here and abroad, and gave me a bad name in my own neighbourhood. Therefore I had to squander [them] and send them abroad, even to distant countries, to Italy, to Barbary, to Poland and to other places; most of them, as good as all, were lost, and I have not seen a perutah from them to this day. Even so I did not withdraw my hand from the commandment I had begun, and I did not leave the work of the Lord. All the time that I had money in my hands to spend ⟨S: "on"⟩ the work of printing — for it is a great one — and as long as time and season did not oppose me, I did not hold back one hour from making the Torah great in Israel in this way, so that near and far would gain merit by it. Immediately after the end of the order of prayer, in the year [5]508 [1748], I began the work needed after it.
