They answered me with joy: "Obviously this need not be said, for even strangers were not prevented from it; I was entitled to do so, just as they allowed Ḥakham Ḥagiz to do such a thing, even though he was not an Ashkenazi. Indeed, they were ready for greater things than this: if I asked of them any favour, they were ready and prepared to do my will." Such and such were their words to me, words of conciliation and goodwill, that I should ask something of them and test their heart, faithful to me. But I asked nothing of them, only that my life be given me at my petition (cf. Esth. 7:3), as mentioned.

And when they had agreed to this, as said, so that I had permission from the people of the community to set up a place of prayer in my house, I spoke first with Ḥakham Ḥagiz, peace be upon him, since my house was near his, and I had been with him in great love since the time in Emden. A special affection from him was known to me, and his covenant with me was faithful, as is seen also from the writings and letters he exchanged with me on Torah matters, some of which are presented in my book She'ilat Ya'avetz and in his book Shetei ha-Leḥem. He, peace be upon him, had rented the dwelling house here in Altona for me before I came here, at a high price, for he, of blessed memory, was not experienced in such things. So at the beginning of my stay here, before Rosh Hashanah [5]493 [1732], I went for all the festival days to the synagogue he had in his house (also of Ashkenazim and following their rite), and I entreated him to keep up a minyan, to pray with ten morning and evening regularly, on all the weekdays too. For if he did so, I would regularly go to his synagogue, for it was good for me with him, and we would be as one all the days; although I had permission from the parnassim and leaders to make a separate place of prayer in my house. And if he would not do so, I would have to make a special place of prayer in my house against my will, for it could not be otherwise: I could not go to the great synagogue, for it was very much trouble for me, and to forgo prayer with the congregation all the days of the week is not fitting at all; it was not acceptable to me. Others of our friends who went to his minyan on Sabbath and festivals had already urged him and asked him to agree to allow prayer with ten in his house regularly on all weekdays as well, so that they would not have to wander hither and thither for prayer all the days of the week; but they did not succeed. And so he sent me away disappointed and made my words of no account (cf. Job 24:25). So I had to make a special place of prayer in my house.

In addition, when I went to his synagogue my eyes saw ⟨S: "every Sabbath"⟩ a fire burning on the stove, which was surely made on the Sabbath for some cooking and hot food, Sabbath after Sabbath all the year. Already when I was in Emden they had told this about him, and I did not believe the report until I came "and my eyes had seen it" (1 Kgs. 10:7). Even so "I laid my hand upon my mouth" (Job 40:4), and no word came out of my mouth. Besides this I saw things from him that were not right in my eyes for an important man. "These things he did, and I kept silence" (Ps. 50:21), for he was a very hot-tempered man, peace be upon him, and a man of anger; and I said, this too is for the good. Even so my love did not move a hair's breadth, in deed or in word; but he counted against me the said sin, of which I am innocent, and sealed evil [b] against the one who "dwelt securely" with him.

Added to this: it happened when I printed my book Leḥem Shamayim that I did not take an approbation from him. I avoided this because I did not want an abundance of praise and exaggeration in titles and virtues, which he would without doubt have drawn out at great length, as was his way, had he written me an approbation as I wished. This is no novelty, for even about a man whom he did not esteem at all — truly not at all — he would write and print praise amounting to lies. And what was hardest for me about him was that he was "one thing in the mouth and another in the heart" (cf. b. Pesaḥim 113b), as became known to me with full clarity while I was in Emden. Through his letters to me he was as one mocking the old head of the court of the Three Communities, R. Y[eḥezkel], mentioned above, and he spoke of him with scorn and contempt even in my ears when I was here with him face to face; he did not cease to speak evil of him and to mock him and his learning, his interpretations, his decisions and his rulings — [and there was made of him] "enough contempt and wrath" (cf. Esth. 1:18) — while to his face he showed him great affection, and presented Torah novellae in his name in books he brought to press, to find favour in the eyes of the head of the court, quoting and mentioning him with such excessive praise that it left nothing for those who truly have the holy spirit. Such things were loathsome to me and like a mockery in my eyes.

But his love was whole at first, as my ears heard that he spoke of me and praised me greatly behind my back — needless to say in writing, as I wrote some of his letters to me in the book She'ilat Ya'avetz — magnifying my praise in great abundance, until time separated us a little, from its said beginning, as written above. At the beginning of my stay in Emden, after he had first visited me by letter to greet me and bless me, I informed him of the complaint and the slander that the country spread about him: that he allowed himself to give free rein to his pen, and printed bitter things about great authors in Israel too — besides that it was a light thing for him to despise and shame the great and old sages of the generation, whose ankles he did not reach, "with talk that does not profit" (cf. So from then on the covenant of my love with him was doubled and bound with the cord of longing — although I had known him before, since the time he lived in Amsterdam, young as I was, and I knew "all the travail that had befallen him" (Num. 20:14) in the war of the serpent Ḥayon ⟨S: "may the name of the wicked rot"⟩, when he fought the war of the Lord with all his heart and suffered much over this matter.
