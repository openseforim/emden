Therefore they did not seek to go on with the householders in general, as they had thought, since he had already been defeated in the war and had fallen, plundered, without rising. But I answered them: "Let not him that girds on boast" (1 Kgs. 20:11), "and do not boast of tomorrow" etc. [a] (Prov. 27:1); and I had already understood that their words were not at all according to proper judgment. Therefore, I said, one must gird oneself with strength for the war — and not for my own sake only do I say it, but also for the sake of the root of the matter, namely to cut down the rising tree ⟨S: "in the exile"⟩ that harms the many, which turns about in his intrigues, and his helpers are many for evil. And I see that you have not fulfilled your duty, to set out your claims and your demands against him properly; only lightly have you held burning coals in your hands, and gone round and round in a circle, and not come to the centre — for I was not here in all this, and they did not inform me in Amsterdam what they were doing, when they presented their strong arguments in the court of the king, may his majesty be exalted. Therefore "the treacherous dealer deals treacherously, and the spoiler spoils" (Isa. 21:2), "like the piercings of a sword" (Prov. 12:18) — a wicked man and a sinner. All this their foolish counsel caused; "and the sons of iniquity afflicted more than at first" (cf. 2 Sam. 7:10), and were brazen-faced to fall upon me suddenly to plunder my tents. And even if this time too the Lord did not let them harm me, even so they caused great expense, the loss of much money for nothing, and very great worry; and "had it not been the Lord who was for us" (Ps. 124:1), "then they would have swallowed us alive" (Ps. 124:3).

This was when I had rested at home a little. Many of our friends entreated me to write, in the manner of a responsum, on the subject of the quarrel for the Lord and the said case ⟨S: "of the troubler"⟩, and I did as I was commanded. They also asked me to compose a general letter to all the rabbis and sages of the communities, for our friends to come and sign, to make known the certainty of the words of truth (Prov. 22:21), how they had dealt uprightly with him ⟨S: "with the said wicked one, who conspires against the Lord"⟩. In this too I did not turn aside from their commands; I did not transgress, and I did not delay in doing their will. I wished to fulfil what they asked; I hurried and was diligent; but they were slothful in the matter, and did neither this nor that.

Meanwhile there came out in print the book of ⟨S: "the apostate"⟩ Karl Anton mentioned above, whose author is Eybeschütz himself, as said; and he wrote to the king, may his majesty be exalted, to ask him to stand at his right hand, since he shows a friendly face to all men ⟨S: "to the nations of the world"⟩. In the end, at Ḥanukkah [5]513 [1752], the word of the king went out concerning Eybeschütz: that the power and the honour were taken from him, so that he could no longer compel any man to stand in judgment before him, as before, when the rod and the strap had been in the hand of the head of the court of the Three Communities — this power was taken from him; but the rest of the office of head of the court was left to him. And a decree was decreed that no one should speak any more of the matter of the amulets, nor disgrace or condemn him, on pain of a great fine.

But our party did not let its hands slacken because of this, and went back to accept the judgment of the king, may his majesty be exalted. And not only this, but some known men from the other side joined them; for they saw the intrigues of the deceitful party, who at first had promised that in any case he would clear himself in a Jewish court before the sages of the generation, if only they would first help him in the court of the nations; and after he had been acquitted, then his righteousness would be revealed to the eyes of all, according to the law of Moses and of Judaism. And when they had trusted in his deceitful words and had done their part in the court of the king, may his majesty be exalted — how much money and how many lives they sank in this vain building! — they had laboured in vain. They came to him and said to him that it was now incumbent on him to do his part too, to keep his promise to them, to clear his merit in a Jewish court before middle-of-the-road rabbis whom they would choose. Then he laughed and mocked them, and sent them away empty. And now I will return to my own events. On 8 Iyyar [5]513 [1753] my wife bore again, and bore me a son, and I called him Levi, after the event in which I was tried in the quarrel for the Lord, as it is written: "And of Levi he said" etc. "whom you tried" etc. (Deut. 33:8). My friends rejoiced on the day of his circumcision, also on the holy Sabbath (?). But for our sins he too did not survive, like his first brother, who was also born on the same day of the month, as said; and he died in the year [5]514 [1754], on 18 Av. After that she bore me a daughter, in the following year, on the same day, in the month of Av [5]515 [1755], for good luck; I called her Ḥannah, may she live long and well, and may I merit to see her joy with the rest of the living.

And a great miracle happened with this child while she was still in her mother's womb, two weeks before the birth. For at that time, on 3 Av, on the holy Sabbath, the band of rioters came to my house to search for the A. A. [b]; and I alone was in the house with the lad, the printer Moses Bun, may he live, on the upper floor, and my wife and my son and my daughters on the lower floor. Suddenly the robbers came upon us at midday, on purpose, for they reckoned that no man would then be found staying with me; and they fell upon us like locusts. And my wife, who did not know what this insolence was about, almost had her soul fly away, and had to be bled; the people of my household too were like dead, until the rioters went away and the spirit returned; they too had to be bled. Indeed, to tell this event in its order and as it happened needs a separate booklet, to tell "the praises of the Lord, and His strength, and His wonders" (Ps. 78:4) that He did with us on that day; "you would not believe it if it were told" (Hab. 1:5). It was not one miracle, but perhaps ten tangible miracles were done; with the eye one saw the wonderful and awesome providence of the Blessed Name over those who trust in Him with all their heart. Blessed be the Name, that He did not harm my wife at all; and my daughter, the said child, was born two weeks after this, whole and healthy, with a thread of grace drawn over her (cf. b. Ḥagigah 12b), and she finds favour in the eyes of all who see her (cf. Esth. 2:15). As pleasant as her name, so may she be, and may she live long, in good years.
