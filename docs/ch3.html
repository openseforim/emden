Levi. And there was a great woman there, distinguished and very intelligent, the daughter of the leader, the learned R. L[evi], peace be upon him, and the daughter-in-law of the said leader. She took me in to eat bread and to lodge in her house. She acted valiantly in her wisdom to speak to the heart of her father-in-law and her husband and all the members of the community; in general she strove with all her strength to keep me with them. They told me nothing, but acted at once: they gathered the whole community and agreed with one mind, and accepted me on the intermediate days of the festival of Passover — for I had come there for the festival of Passover, to stay until after the time of the festival and to go on from there immediately after Passover — and appointed me over them as head, rabbi and teacher. Suddenly the messengers of the community came to me and informed me of their agreement, and many entreated me to grant their request.

And I let myself be found by them and by their request, although it was against the desire of my heart, as said above; but I saw with my own eyes that it was from Heaven, since it had never entered my mind at all, and I had not approached a single one of them about this matter, and the Lord put into them one heart and one mind, by their own awakening, willingly and eagerly. They also needed a teacher, for they were "like sheep without a shepherd" (Num. 27:17). I said: "Is it not a little one?" (Gen. 19:20) — I could apply myself to my study as my heart desired without hindrance, and with this I would bring merit to the many, with God's help. I wrote to my wife, peace be upon her, to come to me to Emden with my children, may they live, and all that I had. And the Holy One, blessed be He, brought them as "on eagles' wings" (Ex. 19:4), whole and well: my wife the rabbanit and my two sons Meir and Zalman, may they live — the first, my firstborn, was then about twelve years old, the second about six — and my daughter, may she live, a little girl, with household goods, movables and books, from a very long way ⟨S: "without any mishap"⟩, at the beginning of the summer of [5]489 [1729]. Praise and thanks to the Master of kindness and wonders, blessed be He "who does great things past finding out" (Job 9:10) and awesome things!

But the said sufferings from the urine, though they had lessened a little, still went on tormenting me and troubling me day and night; "my sleep fled from my eyes" (Gen. 31:40) and "my soul was sore troubled" (Ps. 6:4). My wife, peace be upon her, was also in great distress on my account over this, for hardly any vigour rose in me, as if "the caperberry had failed" (Eccl. 12:5), God forbid; and so I had no offspring from her all the days that I stayed in Emden. Perhaps the cause was also the sickness and ailment of my wife, peace be upon her, which came upon her anew there, as written above. But before I moved my dwelling from there the Lord gave her conception, and she bore a son in Altona.

For all that, I was diligent at my study, and "the night shone for me like the day" (Ps. 139:12), with God's help, in Scripture, Mishnah, Gemara, Rashi and Tosafot, halakhot and decisors, and the other holy books of the early authorities, revealed and hidden; and regular lessons "in their order" with students, young men of the city, and my sons, may they live. I also "filled my hand to the Lord" (Ex. 32:29) with "the scribe's inkhorn" (Ezek. 9:2), to make handles for the Torah (cf. b. Eruvin 21b) in some works, some of which I had already begun in Brod. I led the community for the sake of Heaven; thank God, I made many excellent and important ordinances for them, to remove the stumbling-blocks over which they used to stumble before, as some of them are mentioned in the letter I wrote on the matter to R. M. Ḥ. [Moses Ḥagiz], an answer to his letter in which he informed me that [the man] complained of me that I was far too strict with them, "excess of dignity" (Gen. 49:3); see there. I did judgment and justice for the needy with all my strength and ability, without price. I also taught students, sons of exceedingly rich men, and would accept nothing from them as payment for teaching — even from the son of the leader R. Jonathan Levi the elder, who was a very rich man, worth at the least a hundred thousand. He sent me a fine gift because his son studied with me, and I would not accept it. Even for the sermons I preached to them on the Great Sabbath and the Sabbath of Return I did not take a single penny, although their custom before this was to give 22 Reichstaler for each sermon, and although they took great pleasure in my preaching, and each time I spoke at length for many hours, and each sermon is like a small work in itself, and my words were very sweet to them. The intelligent among them swore — [even men who had studied] in the yeshiva of Prague [a] — that their ears had never heard such wonderful and delightful sermons as they heard from me, and they praised me far and wide, saying there is no preacher in the world like me.

But for my part it was "a provocation of my anger and my fury" (Jer. 32:31) to waste time on the study of homiletics, with which I had never occupied myself in my life except out of necessity. For nothing was sweet to my soul but the study of Gemara and decisors, and understanding Scripture and Midrashim, to know and to gain insight in the holy Torah, "to keep, to do" and to fulfil — not to be proud, God forbid, much less to adorn myself with them before people, which was abhorrent to me — but only to grow wise in the Torah for the perfection of my soul alone. "My soul breaks for the longing" (Ps. 119:20) at all times; "in her love be ravished always" (Prov. 5:19). Nevertheless, with God's help, I saw a good sign in my study, also in the study of homiletics and novellae on aggadah: when I needed them, the Blessed One in His kindness provided me with fine and delightful words, sweet and pleasant to the hearers and close to the truth. I have no doubt that the Blessed One graciously let me clothe many wondrous aggadot, very strange at first glance, in adorned garments and ornaments more precious than fine gold. "I will sing to the Lord, for He has dealt bountifully with me" (Ps. 13:6), and I will give thanks to Him, blessed be He, for my portion.

I will further mention, about my conduct with the community, that I showed no favour to the great and was "not afraid of the face of any man" (Deut. 1:17); small and great, poor and rich, were equal in my eyes. Needless to say, I was beloved by all the people of my community, from small to great; I was in their eyes like an angel of God before them; no one rebelled against my word and no one opposed even a hint of mine. Only one man was something of an adversary to me at the beginning of my stay, and a thorn in it. For the leader R. Even after this, in the days of her pregnancy, the flow of blood returned to her in great abundance, very much, like a rushing stream, so that her soul almost flew away in a faint because of the great loss of blood, and they had already given up hope; yet the Blessed One saved her from death. This happened to her several times in Altona while she was still pregnant. The first time she used the remedies of the physician R. Simḥah. I showed him the prescriptions that the doctor in Leiden had drawn up for her, and when he saw them he stood astonished and said that he had put her in great danger; he wondered greatly that she had been able to endure that remedy, which carried enormous risk and was closer to harm than to benefit. But his remedies did not help her either, for after a short time the flow of blood assailed her again, as mentioned, and her hope was almost lost — had it not been for the kindnesses of the Lord, who heard the voice of our cry that went up on high; and she returned to her strength. Afterwards she bore a whole male child; but for our many sins he did not survive, though he had already reached his seventh year, as said above. After two years she bore my daughter Esther, may she live, and after her she bore me two more daughters, who did not survive. There remained to me from her only my two sons the rabbis, may their light shine, and two daughters, may they live. Two little sons died to me in Moravia, and two daughters in Altona in her lifetime, and a son and a daughter died after her death; so that few were left of many — the Lord be over them, may they live.
