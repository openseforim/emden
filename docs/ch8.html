Therefore I agreed to buy the houses adjoining mine, and I sank into them almost all the money in my hand, about fifteen hundred Sh.K.; and I still lacked four thousand for the first payment. For half a year I sought them and did not find them, as it seems because of my enemies without cause, who had made my savour stink with all the gentile moneyed men who lend their money on houses. When the time came and I could not raise it, I had to seek a loan for the time being from my friends. I took great trouble with them and begged them to save me from the disgrace of the court and from useless expense, but their ear was too heavy to hear, although in truth it was a small matter for them. For there were at least four men, distinguished leaders, whom I asked for this, namely the parnas and leader R. G[ershon] K[at]z, may he live, the parnas and leader R. Y. Hozen, may he live, the parnas and leader R. M. G. Sh., and the leader R. Y. Göttingen, may he rest in peace. I wanted to do it with them by way of a permitted partnership (heter iska), and for a short time, until I found a gentile lender; but they would not. All of them hung it on the last of the said men, for he was "rich in public" and "master of wheat", for whom everyone needs to produce cash (cf. b. Horayot 14a), while for him it was a light and small thing, especially on so secure a pledge, with God's help. Yet all the requests and intercessions, mine and others', were in vain, for he was a hard man — saving his honour — and he left me in this great distress, so that I was close to losing the money I had already sunk into this purchase, besides the contempt and the shame and the joy of my opponents; until he saw that "the waters had come up to the soul" (Ps. 69:2), for the date of payment had already passed and I was summoned to court. Then his compassion was kindled; but in anger and wrath he forced himself to grant me my request this time, after he had caused a needless expense of about twenty Reichstaler. Then the said four men gave the said sum in partnership at interest of "4 p.c." [a], and I repaid them the thousand Reichstaler; and of this loan of theirs a thousand Sh.K. remained [owing] to the said four men, without payment of interest.

At that time rows of stones fell from the top of the wall of my dwelling house on the street side, and I made a great new expense on my house, to renew it completely inside and out, and to set at the edges of the roof on the street side gutters of hewn stone with a fine iron railing, in place of the wooden gutters and railing it had before, so that the new expense on it alone came to about a hundred Reichstaler; besides what I also spent on the houses I had recently bought, to repair all their breaches as needed.
