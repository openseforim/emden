  • P. 204, line 10: one should remark that from here one sees how the gaon Ya'avetz meditated day and night in the Torah of the Lord. Once he sat meditating on holy books in the middle of the night, and sleep fell upon his eyelids, with the candle standing before him; and a coal fell from the burning candle onto the books and writings he was occupied with, and the fire ate around them; and since his sleep was sweet to him he noticed nothing. Several writings and leaves were burnt and turned to ash, and the flame almost took hold of the hair of his head, when Jacob awoke from his sleep and trembled exceedingly. When his soul returned to him, he knew the Lord's kindness upon him, who had saved him and his writings; and those that were burnt were the very ones that had already been printed, and the remainder, which had not yet come out to the light of the world, was "a brand plucked out of the fire". Behold a miracle within a miracle! See the book Mor u-Ketzi'ah (part 1, at the end, fol. 103).
  • P. 211, line 23: "the rabbi R. I." — on the preceding page is written of him "our teacher R. Yitzḥak", and the truth is that he was called R.
